# Inspection Cadence: Forecasts & QBRs

## Purpose
This framework defines who inspects what, how often, and at what level of detail as a forecast or a book of business moves up a sales organization — from a front-line rep's pipeline to a Global CRO's board commit. It builds directly on the [Sales Forecasting](sales-forecasting.md) framework (MEDDICC qualification, manager forecast vs. roll-up) and the [QBR Template](qbr-template.pptx), and applies both consistently across every layer of the org rather than treating them as a Sales-only or top-of-house-only exercise.

The core principle: **every layer of the org should run the same two motions — a forecast inspection and a business review — at a cadence and altitude appropriate to that layer.** A front-line rep inspects individual deals weekly. A Global CRO inspects regional trends quarterly. Same underlying discipline, different zoom level.

## 1. Example Org Structure

```
Global CRO
    │
    ├── Regional MD — AMER
    │       ├── Sales Director (Enterprise)
    │       │       ├── Sales Manager
    │       │       │       └── Front Line Reps (SALs)
    │       │       └── Sales Manager
    │       │               └── Front Line Reps (SALs)
    │       └── Sales Director (Commercial/Mid-Market)
    │               ├── Sales Manager
    │               │       └── Front Line Reps (SALs)
    │               └── Sales Manager
    │                       └── Front Line Reps (SALs)
    │
    ├── Regional MD — EMEA
    │       └── (same Sales Director → Sales Manager → Front Line structure)
    │
    └── Regional MD — APAC
            └── (same Sales Director → Sales Manager → Front Line structure)
```

| Layer | Typical span of control | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Global CRO** | 3 Regional MDs | Global bookings number, board/investor forecast commit |
| **Regional MD** | 2–4 Sales Directors | Regional bookings number, cross-functional (Marketing/CX) alignment for the region |
| **Sales Director** | 3–6 Sales Managers | Segment or sub-region bookings number (e.g., Enterprise, Commercial) |
| **Sales Manager** | 6–10 Front Line reps | Team bookings number, individual rep deal coaching |
| **Front Line (SAL)** | Individual book of business | Individual quota, deal-level MEDDICC qualification |

---

## 2. The Two Motions, at Every Layer

| Motion | What it is | Primary tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Forecast Inspection** | A recurring, deal-evidence-based check on whether the number will land | MEDDICC deal inspection, manager forecast vs. roll-up |
| **Business Review** | A periodic, broader look at performance, pipeline health, segment/territory trends, and cross-functional dependencies | QBR (or a scaled-down version of it) |

Forecast inspection happens more often and looks at fewer, more granular things. Business review happens less often and looks at more, broader things. Every layer does both — the cadence and depth just change.

## 3. Inspection Cadence by Layer

### 3.1 Front Line (SAL)

| Cadence | Activity | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Daily/ad hoc | Deal-level CRM hygiene — MEDDICC fields updated as evidence changes, not batched at week's end | Self-managed |
| Weekly | 1:1 deal inspection with Sales Manager on every Commit/Best Case deal | 30–45 min, MEDDICC pillar walk-through (see Sales Forecasting §3.3) |
| Monthly | Individual pipeline coverage check against quota | Self-serve from Pipeline Coverage Calculator |

**No QBR at this layer** — front-line reps feed the Sales Manager's QBR input but don't run their own.

### 3.2 Sales Manager

| Cadence | Activity | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | 1:1 deal inspections with each rep (see above) | 30–45 min per rep |
| Weekly | Submits **team roll-up** (mechanical sum of rep categorizations) and **team manager forecast** (independent, evidence-based number) to Sales Director | CRM/forecast tool submission |
| Bi-weekly | Team pipeline generation and coverage review | Pipeline Coverage Calculator, team view |
| Quarterly | Team-level mini-QBR — feeds into Sales Director's QBR rather than standing alone | Internal team meeting, informal deck or shared doc |

### 3.3 Sales Director

| Cadence | Activity | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Reviews each Sales Manager's roll-up vs. manager forecast; drills into variance outliers | Forecast call, 45–60 min |
| Weekly | Submits **segment/sub-region roll-up** and **segment manager forecast** to Regional MD | CRM/forecast tool submission |
| Bi-weekly | Deal reviews on largest/highest-risk opportunities across all teams (typically top 10–20 deals by ARR) | MEDDICC-based deal review, cross-team |
| Monthly | Capacity and territory health check (open territories, ramp status, attrition) | Capacity Model, Hiring Plan tab |
| Quarterly | Runs segment-level QBR — rolls up into Regional MD's QBR | QBR Template (Segment Performance, Win/Loss, Challenges & Risks sections) |

### 3.4 Regional MD

| Cadence | Activity | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Reviews each Sales Director's roll-up vs. manager forecast; submits **regional roll-up** and **regional manager forecast** to Global CRO | Forecast call, 45–60 min |
| Bi-weekly | Cross-functional alignment check with regional Marketing and CX leads (pipeline generation, retention/renewal risk) | Joint review |
| Monthly | Regional forecast accuracy retro — actual close vs. prior forecast, by Sales Director | Sales Ops-facilitated |
| Quarterly | Runs full regional QBR — Sales, Marketing, and CX sections all populated for the region | QBR Template (full deck) |

### 3.5 Global CRO

| Cadence | Activity | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Reviews all three regional roll-ups vs. regional manager forecasts; forms the **global forecast commit** | Global forecast call, 60 min |
| Monthly | Global forecast accuracy retro across all regions; identifies systemic vs. regional-specific variance patterns | Sales Ops-facilitated |
| Quarterly | Consolidates regional QBRs into the board/executive business review | Executive-level summary, references each region's QBR |
| Annually | Owns segmentation, territory, and capacity model sign-off ahead of the new fiscal year | Segmentation Framework, Territory Planning, Capacity Model |

## 4. How Forecast Numbers Flow Upward

At every layer, the same manager-forecast-vs-roll-up discipline from the Sales Forecasting framework applies — it just compounds as it moves up:

```
Front Line categorizes deals (MEDDICC-based)
        ↓
Sales Manager: Team Roll-Up  +  Sales Manager's own Manager Forecast
        ↓
Sales Director: Roll-Up of Sales Manager forecasts  +  Director's own Manager Forecast
        ↓
Regional MD: Roll-Up of Director forecasts  +  MD's own Manager Forecast
        ↓
Global CRO: Roll-Up of Regional MD forecasts  +  CRO's own Manager Forecast (board commit)
```

**Important:** at each layer, the "roll-up" going upward is the sum of the layer below's *manager forecasts*, not their raw CRM roll-ups. This means by the time a number reaches the Global CRO, it has already passed through four independent layers of MEDDICC-based judgment — not just four layers of arithmetic. The CRO's own forecast should still be independently formed, not a rubber stamp of what rolled up, for the same reason a Sales Manager's forecast shouldn't just restate their reps' numbers (see Sales Forecasting §3.2–3.3).

**Variance tracking should happen at every layer, not just at the top.** A Sales Director whose forecast is consistently far from their Sales Managers' combined forecast is exhibiting the same signal (miscalibration, or genuine additional insight) that a CRO vs. Regional MD variance would show — it should be reviewed at that level, not only when it reaches the top.

## 5. QBR Cadence and Roll-Up

QBRs follow the same "each layer's output feeds the next layer's input" logic as forecasts, but on a quarterly cycle:

| Layer | QBR scope | Rolls into |
|---|---|---|
| Sales Manager | Team performance, individual coaching themes (informal, not board-visible) | Sales Director's QBR |
| Sales Director | Segment/sub-region performance, win/loss themes, territory health | Regional MD's QBR |
| Regional MD | Full regional QBR — Sales + Marketing + CX, using the standard QBR Template | Global CRO's consolidated review |
| Global CRO | Cross-regional synthesis — highlights, systemic risks, asks for the board/exec team | Board/executive business review |

**Suggested quarterly timeline:**

| Week (of quarter close) | Activity |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Sales Manager mini-QBRs with their Sales Director |
| Week 1–2 | Sales Director QBRs with their Regional MD |
| Week 2 | Regional MD finalizes full regional QBR (Sales + Marketing + CX) |
| Week 2–3 | Regional MDs present to Global CRO |
| Week 3 | Global CRO consolidates into board/executive review |

This staggered cadence means each layer has real data from the layer below before presenting up, rather than every layer scrambling to build a QBR from scratch off the same raw CRM export simultaneously.

## 7. Common Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Skipping layers.** A Global CRO who inspects front-line deals directly (or a Sales Director who never actually reviews a Sales Manager's forecast logic) breaks the chain of independent judgment this whole model depends on.
- **Treating every layer's QBR as a from-scratch exercise.** If Regional MDs aren't building on Sales Director QBRs that already happened, the org is duplicating analysis under time pressure instead of synthesizing it.
- **Only reviewing forecast variance at the top.** By the time a mismatch is visible to the CRO, it's often too late in the quarter to fix. Variance should be a standing agenda item at every layer's weekly forecast call.
- **Conflating forecast inspection with business review.** A weekly forecast call is not the place for segment strategy discussion, and a quarterly QBR is too infrequent to catch a slipping deal. Keep the two motions distinct.
- **No consequence loop.** Monthly forecast accuracy retros only add value if they feed back into coaching (front line), calibration (managers/directors), or process changes (Sales Ops) — not just into a scorecard nobody acts on.
