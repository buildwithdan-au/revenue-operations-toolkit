# Sales Forecasting: A MEDDICC-Based Framework

## Purpose
This framework defines how to forecast sales outcomes with confidence grounded in deal qualification rigor rather than rep optimism. It uses [MEDDICC](https://meddicc.com/) — Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion, Competition — as the qualification backbone for every forecast category, and treats the gap between **rep roll-ups** and **manager forecasts** as one of the most useful leading indicators a sales org has, rather than a discrepancy to paper over.

---

## 1. Why MEDDICC as the Forecasting Foundation

Most forecast inaccuracy doesn't come from bad math — it comes from forecasting on deals that were never properly qualified in the first place. A stage in the CRM tells you where a deal *sits*; it doesn't tell you whether the deal is *real*. MEDDICC closes that gap by forcing every deal to answer seven questions before it's allowed to carry forecast weight:

| Pillar | What it answers | Forecasting relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **M**etrics | What quantified economic value does the customer expect? | No metrics = no urgency = slips |
| **E**conomic Buyer | Who can actually authorize spend, and have we engaged them directly? | Deals without EB access are the single biggest source of forecast slippage |
| **D**ecision Criteria | What technical/business criteria will the buyer score vendors against? | If criteria are unknown, win probability is a guess, not an assessment |
| **D**ecision Process | What are the actual steps, gatekeepers, and timeline to a signed contract? | Undefined process is why "this quarter" deals become "next quarter" deals |
| **I**dentify Pain | What specific, senior-validated business pain is driving the purchase? | Vague pain = a deal that competes with "do nothing" |
| **C**hampion | Do we have an internal advocate with the influence and motivation to sell for us when we're not in the room? | No champion = no visibility into what's really happening internally |
| **C**ompetition | Who/what are we competing against, including internal status quo? | Unknown competitive position inflates win-rate assumptions |

A deal can be "Commit" in a rep's head and still fail every pillar above. The forecasting framework's job is to make that visible before the deal slips, not after.

## 2. Forecast Categories Defined by MEDDICC Completeness

Rather than letting forecast category be a subjective judgment call, tie each category to a minimum bar of **validated** (not just asserted) MEDDICC evidence. "Validated" means confirmed by the customer or corroborated by a second source — not simply written in the CRM by the rep.

| Forecast Category | MEDDICC Bar | Typical Win Rate Range* |
|---|---|---|
| **Pipeline** | Pain and Metrics identified; EB and Champion not yet confirmed | 10–20% |
| **Best Case** | EB identified (not necessarily engaged); Champion identified; Decision Criteria understood | 25–40% |
| **Commit** | EB directly engaged; Champion validated as actively selling internally; Decision Process and paper/approval path mapped; Competition understood and addressed | 70–90% |
| **Closed Won** | Contract signed | 100% |

*Illustrative ranges — every org should calibrate these against its own historical stage-to-close data rather than adopt a benchmark blindly.

**The discipline this creates:** a rep cannot move a deal to Commit just because it's "supposed to close this quarter." It moves to Commit when the MEDDICC evidence bar is met — full stop. This is what makes forecast categories mean the same thing across every rep and every region.

## 3. Manager Forecasts vs. Roll-Ups

This is the single highest-leverage control in the forecasting process, and the one most orgs under-use.

### 3.1 The two numbers, defined

- **Roll-Up Forecast:** The sum of what reps have entered into the CRM at the opportunity level (their own Commit + Best Case categorization), aggregated mechanically with no adjustment.
- **Manager Forecast:** The number a sales manager (first-line through regional/VP) independently commits to, after reviewing the underlying deals — which may be higher *or* lower than the roll-up.

These should **never** be forced to match. If they always match, the manager forecast is adding no value — it's just re-typing the roll-up.

### 3.2 Why they diverge — and why that's useful

| Pattern | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Manager forecast < Roll-up | **Happy ears** — reps are counting deals on optimism or a single verbal "yes" rather than validated MEDDICC evidence. Manager has seen the deal reviews and doesn't believe the Champion or EB story holds up. |
| Manager forecast > Roll-up | **Sandbagging** — reps are deliberately under-forecasting to protect themselves against a miss, or holding deals in a lower category past the point the evidence supports, to create a quarter-end "surprise" beat. |
| Manager forecast ≈ Roll-up, both wrong at close | **Systemic MEDDICC gap** — the qualification bar itself isn't being enforced in deal reviews, so neither number reflects real deal health. This is a process failure, not a people failure. |

The **variance between the two numbers**, tracked over time, is itself a forecast-quality metric — a manager who is consistently far from their team's roll-up (in either direction) is telling you something about either their team's qualification discipline or their own calibration.

### 3.3 How the manager forecast should be built

The manager forecast is not a vibe check — it's built by **MEDDICC-based deal inspection**, not by asking "are we still good for this deal?" In forecast/deal review calls, managers should challenge each Commit and Best Case deal against the pillars, not against the rep's confidence:

- *Metrics:* "What number did the customer give us, and who gave it to us?"
- *Economic Buyer:* "When did you last speak with the EB directly? What did they say in their own words?"
- *Decision Criteria:* "Where did the criteria come from — a doc they shared, or your assumption?"
- *Decision Process:* "Walk me through every remaining step to signature, with named owners and dates."
- *Identify Pain:* "What happens to the business if they do nothing? Who told you that?"
- *Champion:* "What has the champion done for us, unprompted, in the last two weeks?"
- *Competition:* "Who else is in this deal, and what's our differentiated angle against them specifically?"

Any pillar the rep can't answer with a specific, corroborated fact is a pillar that isn't actually validated — and the deal's category should reflect that, regardless of the rep's confidence or the deal's stage in the CRM.

### 3.4 Escalation and reconciliation cadence

| Cadence | Activity | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Rep-level deal inspection on all Commit/Best Case deals using MEDDICC pillars | First-line manager |
| Weekly | First-line manager submits independent manager forecast (roll-up visible but not binding) | First-line manager |
| Bi-weekly | Regional/VP forecast call — reviews roll-up vs. manager forecast variance by team, drills into outliers | Regional/VP Sales Leader |
| Monthly | Forecast accuracy retro — actual close vs. both roll-up and manager forecast, by rep and by manager | Sales Ops |
| Quarterly | Recalibrate forecast category win-rate bands against actual historical conversion | Sales Ops |

The regional/VP layer should specifically interrogate **large variances** between a manager's forecast and their team's roll-up — not to force alignment, but to understand whether the manager has information the CRM doesn't (a very healthy reason for divergence) or whether deal reviews aren't happening with enough rigor (an unhealthy one).

## 4. Metrics to Track Forecast Health

- **Forecast accuracy** — actual closed bookings vs. Commit-category forecast, by rep, manager, and region, tracked over rolling quarters
- **Roll-up vs. manager variance** — tracked as its own trend line, not just at quarter-end but week-by-week through the quarter
- **Category migration** — how often deals slip backward (Commit → Best Case) vs. progress forward, and how late in the quarter slippage happens
- **MEDDICC completeness score** — % of Commit-category pipeline with all seven pillars validated (not just present as CRM fields)
- **Sandbagging/happy-ears index** — frequency and direction of manager overrides vs. roll-up, by manager, to spot individual calibration issues

---

## 5. Common Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Forecasting off CRM stage instead of MEDDICC evidence** — stage tells you where a deal is in a process; it doesn't tell you if the process reflects reality.
- **Treating the manager forecast as a rubber stamp of the roll-up** — if it always matches, it isn't adding the independent judgment it exists to provide.
- **Punishing managers for forecasts that differ from the roll-up** — the variance is signal, not a compliance failure; investigate before correcting.
- **No consequence for repeated happy-ears categorization** — if Commit deals routinely don't close, and the pattern traces to specific reps or managers, that needs direct coaching, not just a lower number next quarter.
- **MEDDICC as a CRM form rather than a conversation discipline** — filled-in fields that were never actually validated with the customer are worse than empty fields, because they create false confidence.
- **No historical calibration** — using generic win-rate-by-category benchmarks indefinitely instead of tuning them to the org's own actual conversion data.

---

## 6. Suggested Rollout Sequence

| Step | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Define MEDDICC validation bar for each forecast category (Section 2) and socialize with all sales leaders |
| 2 | Train reps and managers on MEDDICC deal inspection questions (Section 3.3) — this is a coaching skill, not a form-filling exercise |
| 3 | Stand up parallel roll-up and manager forecast submission in the CRM/forecasting tool |
| 4 | Run 1–2 quarters tracking variance without penalizing divergence, to build a baseline |
| 5 | Introduce monthly forecast accuracy retros (Section 3.4) and use them to recalibrate win-rate bands |
| 6 | Fold recurring variance patterns into manager coaching and rep enablement, not just forecast adjustment |

---

*This framework assumes the segmentation and territory structures already in place — MEDDICC qualification bars and win-rate bands should ultimately be validated per segment (Enterprise deals typically need more Decision Process rigor than SMB, for example) rather than applied as one flat standard across the business.*
