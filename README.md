# README: My Approach to Sales Forecasting & Pipeline Health

**Owner:** Dan McHugh
**Scope:** Sales Forecasting, Inspection Cadence, KPI Dictionary, Pipeline Health Framework, Sales Velocity Model

## What I bring to the table

Most sales organizations at $50M–$2B ARR have a forecasting *process* — a CRM, some categories, a weekly call. Very few have a forecasting *system*, where deal-level qualification, management judgment, org-wide cadence, shared metric definitions, and lever-based diagnosis are all built to reinforce each other rather than exist as five separate spreadsheets that quietly disagree by Q2.

That's what I build. The five artifacts in this set aren't five independent tools — they're one connected answer to a single question: **"How do I know, with evidence rather than confidence, whether we're going to hit the number — and if we're not, exactly where the problem is and who should be fixing it?"**

## The problem I'm solving for

Forecast misses are rarely a math problem. They're almost always one of these, compounding quietly until they show up in a quarter-end shortfall:

- Deals get categorized on rep confidence instead of validated evidence
- A manager's forecast is just a re-typed version of the roll-up, adding no independent judgment
- Everyone reviews coverage ratio and calls the pipeline "healthy" without checking the five other things that actually determine whether it converts
- Sales, Marketing, and CX each quote a metric with the same name and a different formula behind it
- Nobody can say which lever — volume, win rate, deal size, or cycle time — actually moves the number, so improvement plans default to "sell harder"

I build the connective tissue that closes each of those gaps, and I build it so each layer of the organization inspects it at a cadence appropriate to that layer — not as a once-a-quarter fire drill.

## The principles behind how I work

**Evidence over confidence.** A deal's forecast category should reflect what's been validated with the customer, not how the rep feels about it. This is the spine of the MEDDICC-based forecasting approach and the Pipeline Health Framework's Quality dimension.

**Variance is signal, not noise.** When a manager's forecast doesn't match the roll-up beneath it, that's not a compliance problem to correct — it's information about sandbagging, happy ears, or genuine judgment the CRM doesn't capture. I build processes that surface and use that variance rather than force it to zero.

**One number is never the whole story.** Coverage ratio, on its own, is one of six things that determine whether a pipeline is actually healthy. Bookings attainment, on its own, doesn't tell you whether Marketing's pipeline contribution or CX's retention performance are pulling their weight. I build frameworks that force the second, third, and fourth question, not just the first.

**Diagnosis should point at a lever, not a vibe.** "Improve pipeline health" is not an action plan. "Reduce Enterprise sales cycle length by 15 days, which the sensitivity model shows is worth more than an equivalent increase in deal count" is. Every framework in this set is built to end in a specific, prioritized action, not a general sense of concern.

**Shared definitions are a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.** Cross-functional alignment between Sales, Marketing, and CX fails constantly for the boring reason that everyone's using a slightly different formula for the same-named metric. Fixing that is unglamorous and foundational — which is exactly why I make it its own standalone artifact rather than a footnote.

**The org chart is the delivery mechanism.** A good framework that only lives at the top of the house doesn't change behavior. Every process I design specifies who does what, at what cadence, at every layer from front-line rep to CRO — because that's the difference between a framework and a binder.

## What this enables in practice

- **Forecast calls that interrogate deals, not feelings** — using the MEDDICC pillar questions as the actual script for weekly deal inspection.
- **Earlier warning on at-risk quarters** — because pipeline health is checked on six dimensions weekly at the team level, not one dimension monthly at the top.
- **Faster, more targeted intervention** — the velocity model tells you which lever to pull before you spend a quarter guessing.
- **QBRs that different functions can actually agree on** — because Sales, Marketing, and CX are reading from the same KPI Dictionary going in.
- **A forecast number the CRO can defend to the board** — because it's the product of five independent layers of MEDDICC-based judgment, not one optimistic roll-up passed up the chain untouched.
